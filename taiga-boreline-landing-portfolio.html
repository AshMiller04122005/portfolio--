Your journey through the taiga

  1. You step into the taiga. No roads. No signs. Just wild. An acorn crunches under your boot — the first small sound in a forest that has no end.
  2. The forest closes in. Fir trees rise around you, tall and silent. The branches don't move aside. The taiga doesn't make way for anyone — not even for you.
  3. Something is watching. A deer lifts its head between the trunks. For a moment, you and the forest look at each other. Then it's gone. No trail. No map. Just instinct.
  4. You are not the first here. Paw prints cross the trail — a wolf, or a fox, or something older. You are a guest in this forest, and the taiga has had many guests before you.
  5. The way is blocked. A fallen log lies across the trail, moss-covered and heavy. You climb over it. The forest offers no shortcuts. Every step is earned.
  6. An ocean of green. You climb higher. The trees stretch to the horizon. Somewhere above, a helicopter crosses the sky — small, distant, and somehow out of place in a world this old. The taiga has no end.
  7. You have reached the heart. The forest opens. Mountains rise in the distance. You are almost there — at the center of the largest living forest on Earth. No spam. Just the wild.
